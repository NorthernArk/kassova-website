Families often hold investments and property through partnerships and LLCs taxed as partnerships. Each one files its own return and sends each partner a Schedule K-1, and the partners' own returns and estimated payments depend on those K-1s. The chain is only as fast as its slowest link.

## The sequence

1. **Each partnership closes its books.** Its income, deductions and credits are allocated to partners under the partnership agreement.
2. **The partnership files Form 1065.** The IRS instructions say a domestic partnership generally files by the 15th day of the 3rd month after its tax year ends, and can request an extension.
3. **Partners receive Schedule K-1.** The partnership furnishes each partner a K-1 showing their share.
4. **Partners file their own returns.** A partner who owns an interest in another partnership waits for that one first, so tiered structures wait on the bottom tier.
5. **Estimated payments continue through the year.** The IRS explains that estimated tax is paid in installments during the year on income not subject to withholding, which includes partnership income for many families.

## Where it usually breaks

- **Tiered structures.** A holding partnership cannot finish until the partnerships below it do.
- **Late information from outside managers.** A fund's K-1 can arrive late, holding up everything above it.
- **Estimates set on last year's numbers.** When an entity's income changes sharply, quarterly estimates based on the prior year can be too low or too high.

## Keeping the chain visible

A simple register helps: each entity, what it files, whom it sends K-1s to, from whom it receives them, and the status of each. When the books of each entity are closed on time and the ownership structure is known, the dependencies stop being a surprise each spring.
