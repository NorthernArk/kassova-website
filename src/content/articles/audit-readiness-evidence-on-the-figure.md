Audits, reviews, lender requests and tax examinations all ask a version of the same question: where did this number come from, and who decided it? Audit readiness is being able to answer that in one step.

## What an auditor looks for

Auditing standards, such as the PCAOB's standard on audit evidence, describe evidence in terms of how relevant and reliable it is. In practice that means documents from outside the organisation, such as bank statements, carry weight, and so does a clear record of who prepared and approved an entry.

## Evidence on the figure, not beside it

Most offices have the evidence. It is in a folder, an inbox or a scanner tray, filed by month or by bank rather than against the figure it supports. Being ready means:

- **every imported transaction links to its statement**, so the source is one click away;
- **every manual entry has a reason and an attachment**, written when it was made, not reconstructed later;
- **every entry records who made it and who approved it**;
- **closed periods stay closed**, so the figures reviewed are the figures that were reported.

## A one-hour test

Pick five figures from last quarter's reports at random. For each, find the source document and the names of the preparer and approver. Time it. If any takes more than a few minutes, that is the gap to fix before someone outside asks.

## Readiness is a by-product

The best audit preparation is a close that leaves evidence behind as it goes. Then there is nothing extra to prepare.
