A family office budget is often a spreadsheet copied from last year, adjusted, and then compared with actuals pasted in from the books each month. Every paste is a chance to drift.

## Budget at the level you account at

Set the budget per entity and per period, on the same chart of accounts the ledger uses. Then the comparison is direct: each budget line has actuals from the same accounts, with no mapping table to maintain.

Choose periods that match how the books are kept. Publication 538 explains accounting periods and methods for tax purposes; whatever an entity uses for its books, the budget should use the same periods, or variances will reflect timing rather than spending.

## Read actuals from the ledger

Actuals should come from posted entries, not from an export. When the comparison reads the ledger directly:

- a variance can be traced to the entries behind it;
- a correction to an entry corrects the variance;
- nobody has to remember which version of the export was used.

## Explain, do not just report

A variance report that lists differences is half the job. The useful version groups them:

1. **Timing:** spent earlier or later than planned, no change to the year.
2. **Volume or price:** more or less was used, or it cost more or less.
3. **Unplanned:** something happened that the budget did not foresee.

The owner usually needs only the third group, with a recommendation.

## Roll up by ownership

For a family, a consolidated budget should follow who owns what. When ownership is recorded as a structure rather than a list, the roll-up follows it automatically.
