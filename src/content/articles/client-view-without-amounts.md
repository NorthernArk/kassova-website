Around every family's books there are people who need to know where things stand but not what the numbers are: an assistant who chases missing statements, an adviser coordinating a deadline, a family member who wants to know whether the month is closed. Giving them full access to the figures is more than they need.

## Least privilege, applied to books

Security frameworks call this least privilege: give each person the access their work requires and no more. NIST lists it as a core access control. For a family's books, that often means a role that sees status but no amounts.

## What a status-only view should show

- which entities and periods are closed, and which are open;
- what is missing, such as a statement or an approval, and who holds it;
- decisions waiting for the owner, described without figures;
- deadlines and their owners.

## What it should not show

- balances, transactions or totals;
- documents that contain amounts;
- anything that lets a figure be worked out from the rest.

The last point matters. Hiding a total while showing every line that adds up to it is not hiding anything. A status-only role has to be designed, not achieved by hiding a few columns.

## Why it helps everyone

The controller stops answering "is it done yet?" by email. The family sees progress. And fewer people hold the figures, which is the safest arrangement for a family's financial privacy.
