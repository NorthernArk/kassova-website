Maker and checker is the simplest control there is: one person prepares an entry or a payment, and a different person approves it. Frameworks such as COSO's internal control guidance and NIST's separation-of-duties control describe the same idea at greater length.

## Why it matters more in a small office

In a large company, separation of duties is spread across departments. In a family office of three people, one person often can record a payment, send it and reconcile the account. Nothing has to go wrong on purpose for that to be a risk; a typo with nobody to catch it is enough.

## A version that fits a small team

- **Decide what needs a checker.** Every payment, every manual journal, and any entry above a threshold you choose. Routine imported transactions can be reviewed in bulk.
- **Record both names.** The control is only real if the system records who made the entry and who approved it, and refuses the same person in both roles.
- **Keep approval close to the work.** If approving means finding an email from last Tuesday, approvals get skipped. The item and its evidence should be in front of the checker.
- **Review the exceptions.** Once a month, look at what was approved late, reopened or overridden.

## What it does not do

Maker and checker catches errors and makes misuse harder. It does not replace reconciliation, and it does not help if both people approve without reading. Short, specific items are what keep the checker reading.
