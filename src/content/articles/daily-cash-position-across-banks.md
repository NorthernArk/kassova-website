"How much cash do we have?" is the question a family office is asked most. Across several banks and entities, answering it honestly takes more than adding up balances.

## Start with the list of accounts

A cash position is only complete if it includes every account. Keep one register of accounts: the bank, the owning entity, the currency and the purpose. When a new account opens, it goes on the register first. An account missing from the register is cash missing from the answer.

## Every balance needs a source and a time

A balance without a date is a guess. For each account, the position should show where the figure came from (a statement, a feed, a manual entry) and when that source last arrived. Then a reader can tell "current" from "as of last Thursday" without asking.

## Name the gaps instead of hiding them

When a statement has not arrived, the honest figure is "unknown since Thursday", not zero and not last month's number carried forward silently. A position that labels its stale lines is more useful than one that looks complete and is not.

## From cash to liquidity

Once balances are reliable, set them against what is due: bills, capital calls, tax payments and transfers between entities. The question then becomes the one that matters: can each entity meet what it owes, and from where will the money come if it cannot?

## Keep the evidence

Bank statements are also records you are expected to keep to support your figures. Importing them, rather than retyping balances, gives you both the position and the evidence behind it.
